Body · Neuroscience

Andrew Huberman

Stanford neuroscientist, host of the Huberman Lab podcast — protocols for sleep, light, focus, exercise, and stress.

Quick takeA credentialed, published neuroscientist whose mechanisms are generally sound — but several episodes stretch small or preliminary studies into confident daily protocols, and long-running sponsors (AG1, LMNT, Momentous) sit uncomfortably close to what gets recommended as "the science."

Core techniques worth trying

  • Sleep toolkit numbers — morning light 5 min clear day, 10 min cloudy, 20–30 min overcast, within the first hour of waking, on at least 80% of days (double it the next day if you miss). Caffeine 1–3 mg per kg of body weight per dose, none after about 2–3pm. Hot bath or sauna in the evening capped at 20–30 min drops core temperature 1–3°F afterward and helps sleep onset. Bedroom at least 3°F cooler than daytime. Dim red light under 10 lux is fine at night; total pitch-black is not required.
  • Temperature minimum — your daily low point sits roughly 90 min–2 hr before your usual wake time. Light, exercise, or food in the 4–6 hours after it shifts your clock earlier; in the 4–6 hours before it, later. That's the whole basis of his jet lag and shift-work advice.
  • Physiological sigh — double inhale through the nose, one long exhale through the mouth, 1–3x. Fastest real-time way to calm down; heart rate drop takes ~20–30 seconds.
  • Cold exposure — 45–55°F, 1–5 min, 2–4x/week, ~11 total minutes weekly. Raises dopamine roughly 2.5x, and it stays elevated afterward rather than crashing.
  • 90-minute ultradian work blocks — expect a 5–10 minute ramp-up, cap at 2–3 blocks/day. A 60-second fixed-gaze warm-up beforehand is said to pull mental focus in faster.
  • NSDR (non-sleep deep rest) — 10–30 min, hard cap 90 min. Used after learning to lock it in, midafternoon to reset, or at 3am to get back to sleep.
  • Sauna — 5–20 min at 176–212°F, roughly 1hr/week split into 2–3 sessions for general health; 4–7x/week is the dose linked to lower cardiovascular mortality in the Finnish cohort data he cites.
  • 17-minute interoception meditation — one session measurably reduced "attentional blink" (missed rapid stimuli) even in a single sitting, across ages.
  • Fasting window — 7–9 hours eating window is the practical sweet spot he cites, not an extreme 4–6 hour window.
  • Stress toolkit — exhale-emphasized breathing to calm, inhale-emphasized to energize; weekly "stress inoculation" (cold or hard exercise while deliberately widening your visual gaze to stay calm under real physiological load).

Evidence check

CredentialedHuberman is an active, published Stanford neuroscientist — the mechanism explanations are broadly accurate.

Overreach flaggedScience journalists (Slate, and immunologist Andrea Love) have criticized specific episodes — notably one on immune health — for leaning on very small or non-causal studies to support confident, sellable advice, in segments that doubled as supplement pitches.

SponsorshipAG1, LMNT, and Momentous have sponsored the show since near its start, and Huberman has personal/financial ties to some supplement makers — not proof any specific claim was bought, but a real conflict worth weighing.

More protocol detail (dopamine, learning, hormones, supplements)
  • Dopamine — don't stack multiple dopamine hits onto one activity (music + caffeine + scrolling during a workout depletes the pool faster); vary reward unpredictably rather than on a fixed schedule; avoid routine pre-task stimulants before study, since a big pre-task spike lowers post-task learning.
  • Learning a skill — maximize reps early (errors, not correct reps, open the plasticity window); sit quietly 1–10+ min right after training, no phone, while the brain replays the sequence backward; visualization alone (15 min/day, 5x/week, 12 weeks) produced real but smaller gains than physical practice.
  • Fitness template — alternate monthly between a strength block (4–8 reps, 3–4 sets, 2–4 min rest) and a hypertrophy block (8–15 reps, 2–3 sets, ~90 sec rest); Zone 2 cardio 180–220 min/week; VO2max intervals roughly weekly.
  • Hormones — resistance training in the 1–8 rep range raises testosterone for up to 48 hours; endurance work past ~75 minutes tends to lower it via cortisol; morning light exposure supports the same dopamine-linked pathway.
  • Personal supplement habits (less independently confirmed) — AG1, fish oil, vitamin D3+K2, magnesium, creatine ~5g/day are commonly cited as part of his stack; exact doses vary by source.
  • Skin — mineral sunscreen (zinc oxide, up to 25%) preferred over chemical filters; hydrolyzed collagen 15–30g/day plus vitamin C; reduce alcohol; daily fermented foods for gut-skin links.

Mind · Meditation

Joe Dispenza

Chiropractor turned meditation teacher — mental rehearsal, heart coherence, and "changing your energy" to install a new identity.

Quick takeNot a neuroscientist or physicist, despite the framing — the "quantum field" and consciousness-affects-random-number-generators claims are pseudoscience with no mainstream backing. But strip the cosmology out and the actual practices (breathwork, meditation, gratitude, mental rehearsal) are ordinary, reasonably well-evidenced mind-body tools. Never treat this as a substitute for medical care — there's a documented case of it discouraging chemotherapy.

His routine, and the one he prescribes beginners

  • His stated ideal window is 3–5am, though this is the window he teaches more confidently than a confirmed personal habit. Structure: meditate 45–60 min → move the body → learn something new.
  • Retreats alternate 90-minute meditation blocks with 90-minute lectures, totaling 4–5 hours of meditation a day across a week.
  • For a beginner: start with 15–20 min guided sessions, repeat the same one daily until it feels rote, then switch. Expect the first few days to be a genuine struggle — that's normal, not failure. Give it at least 30 days before judging results. Doing it with an accountability partner is his own suggestion.

Core techniques worth trying

  • Heart coherence breathing — hand on chest, inhale ~5 sec, exhale ~5 sec (about 6 breaths/minute), while holding a real felt emotion of gratitude or love. Start around 10 min; a 1–2 minute version works as a quick reset.
  • Elevated emotion rehearsal — sit with a real memory of gratitude or joy for 5–10 min, several times a day, to change your state going into something before it happens.
  • The 4-step change loop — notice the automatic thought/reaction → interrupt it before it fires → mentally rehearse the new response with real feeling → repeat until it's automatic.
  • Old-self / new-self journaling — one list of specific thoughts/behaviors you no longer choose, one list of the specific ones you're installing instead. Review both regularly, don't write once and forget.
  • Blessing of the energy centers — move attention up through 8 points on the body (base of spine to ~16 inches above the head), pairing each with gratitude or love, then lie down at least 15 minutes afterward. Full practice runs about an hour.
  • Space between thoughts — instead of thinking about your day, rest attention in pure spatial awareness (the space around your body, the room, beyond it) to quiet the analytical mind.

Evidence check

Not a neuroscientistDispenza holds a Doctor of Chiropractic degree; investigative reporting found no support for the "PhD" title used by the unaccredited Quantum University where he also teaches.

Pseudoscience flaggedA 2025 study on his own retreat (published with him as a co-author, and while he's employed by the company that sells the retreat) had no control group and used self-selected, paying attendees already primed by a week of ritual — it can't isolate meditation from expectation or the retreat experience itself. A separate study claims group meditation measurably influenced random-number generators worldwide — this revives a parapsychology research line (the Global Consciousness Project) that was never accepted by mainstream science and was shut down at Princeton in 2007.

Real-world harm documentedJournalists have reported a case where a cancer patient's family considered skipping chemotherapy under the influence of his mind-over-matter framing; he continued treatment and died at 45.

What still holds upMeditation, HRV-linked breathing, gratitude practice, and mental rehearsal all have independent, mainstream support as generic wellness tools — just not the causal story he wraps around them.

More detail (pineal breath, walking meditation, mental rehearsal)
  • Pineal gland breath — slow inhale through the nose while contracting the pelvic floor and drawing that contraction up the spine; hold at the crown ~5–10 sec; release; repeat for 5–10 minutes as a pre-meditation warm-up.
  • Walking meditation ("changing boxes") — stand still, eyes closed, build the coherent-breathing + elevated-emotion state and set an intention; then open your eyes, fix on a distant horizon point, and walk while carrying that feeling into the body.
  • The "river of change" — he frames the discomfort of leaving an old identity as expected and even valuable, not a sign to stop; the antidote is returning to the elevated-emotion practice rather than reverting.
  • Books — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself lays out the thought→feeling→"state of being" loop; You Are the Placebo extends it into belief/expectation; Becoming Supernatural adds the energy-center and heart-coherence material; Evolve Your Brain is the earliest neuroscience-framing book.

Mindset · Goals

Bob Proctor

Self-help teacher (1934–2022), protégé of Earl Nightingale, featured in The Secret — paradigms, goal cards, and self-image.

Quick takeNo formal credentials — a high-school dropout who found Think and Grow Rich at 26 and built a career teaching it. The written-goal and self-image material genuinely overlaps with real psychology. The "Law of Attraction/Vibration" as literal physics, and the claim that disease can't exist in an emotionally healthy body, are pseudoscience. More seriously: Proctor was a paid promoter for two companies (Vemma, iMarketsLive) that the FTC later shut down as illegal pyramid schemes, and his own paid certification program runs $18,000–$32,000.

His daily routine

  • Morning: write a gratitude list of 10 things, first thing. Read Think and Grow Rich — he did this daily for roughly 55 years. Read the goal card aloud. A ~2-minute visualization ("see it, feel it, expect it"). A short guidance meditation. Mentally "send love" to three people who irritate him.
  • Evening: decide the one goal-moving task for tomorrow. A 10-minute reflection — what went well, what to improve. Affirmations right before sleep.

Core techniques worth trying

  • The goal card — on an index card, write "I am so happy and grateful now that ___," completing it in present tense as if already true. Carry it always; read it aloud at minimum on waking and before sleep, more through the day if you can.
  • A-B-C goals — A: you already know how. B: a believable stretch. C: excites and scares you, with no visible "how." Proctor's instruction is to deliberately choose a C goal, because only that size forces real growth.
  • The Terror Barrier — the wall of fear that shows up specifically when you commit to a C goal. His advice: name it for what it is, act while still scared rather than waiting for the fear to pass, and expect it to recur at every new level.
  • Paradigm change — write the opposite of the unwanted pattern in present tense, then repeat it daily with real, manufactured emotion (not flat recitation) for a sustained stretch — commonly cited around 30 days.
  • Self-image (borrowed from Maxwell Maltz's Psycho-Cybernetics) — your results snap back to match your internal self-concept even after external change. The fix is to write and rehearse a new self-image statement, not just try harder at the behavior.

Evidence check

Real psychology underneathLocke & Latham's goal-setting theory — one of the most replicated findings in psychology — genuinely supports writing specific, difficult, present-tense goals over vague ones. The self-image work echoes Bandura's self-efficacy research.

Pseudoscience flaggedThe "Law of Vibration" as a literal causal mechanism, and the claim that disease "cannot live in a body in a healthy emotional state," are not supported by physics or medicine — the second claim risks discouraging real treatment.

Pyramid-scheme tiesProctor was a paid business partner/promoter for Vemma and iMarketsLive (later IM Academy) — both later shut down by the FTC as illegal pyramid schemes. His own Proctor Gallagher Institute sells a "Thinking Into Results" program ($3,500–$13,000) and a consultant certification ($18,000–$32,000+) with a resale structure critics describe as MLM-adjacent.

Bottom lineThe free teaching (goal cards, self-image, repetition) is worth using. Any paid certification tier is a real financial risk, separate from whether the underlying ideas are sound.

More detail (stick-person diagram, six mental faculties)
  • The stick-person diagram — a head split into conscious mind (~5%, can accept or reject an idea) over subconscious mind (accepts and stores whatever is repeated with feeling, then runs it automatically), driving a body that produces behavior and results. The point: to change results, change what you feed the subconscious, not just try harder.
  • Six mental faculties — imagination, intuition, will, memory, reason, perception, taught as trainable one at a time (his "Magic in Your Mind" program addresses one per week).
  • Decision — a real decision is unconditional ("this is my decision," not "I'll try") and made without yet knowing the "how." Expect internal resistance as the old self-image defending itself, not a sign to stop.

Action · Sales & Money

Grant Cardone

Sales trainer and real-estate syndicator — the 10X Rule, massive action, and aggressive follow-up.

Quick takeNot a licensed financial advisor. The sales and follow-up material is the most independently backed part of his teaching — the goal-setting philosophy underneath 10X is a real, evidence-supported idea dressed up in a rhetorical multiplier. His real-estate return claims are a different story: an active federal class action alleges he kept marketing 15%+ returns on his funds after the SEC flagged the basis for that number, with a jury trial set for March 2027. Treat any of his specific investment numbers as unverified until you check the actual filing.

His daily routine

  • Wakes around 6am, described as "beating the sun up." Writes goals by hand immediately on waking.
  • Morning workout; long workdays, commonly cited around 12 hours.
  • Writes the same goals again by hand before sleep — the stated purpose is priming the subconscious at the two highest-leverage windows of the day.
  • Runs his calendar in 15-minute blocks, each tied to a specific target — an empty calendar slot is treated as a warning sign, not free time.

Core techniques worth trying

  • Write goals twice a day — by hand, once on waking, once before sleep, kept visible. He explicitly says to write them more often than twice if it helps.
  • The 10X Rule — set the goal at 10 times what feels realistic, and multiply your planned action to match. His "Four Degrees of Action": do nothing → retreat → normal action → massive action. Only massive action, in his framework, reliably produces outsized results.
  • Agree first — when someone objects, agree with them completely before reframing ("You're right to want to think it over — and that's exactly why...") rather than arguing. He calls this the most-violated rule in selling.
  • The follow-up cadence — his cited statistic: roughly 80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th contact, while most people quit after one or two. His prescription: about 12 touches spread across 6 months, varying the channel each time, always proposing the next specific date before ending contact.
  • Massive-action quota — take your honest current daily outreach number and multiply it by 10 as this week's target, in a protected daily time block.

Evidence check

Backed by dataThe follow-up persistence statistics are genuinely supported by independent sales research — this is arguably his most defensible, least-hyped claim.

Active litigationPino v. Cardone Capital — a federal class action alleging his social-media marketing (15%+ annual returns, "$100K turns into $500/month") misled investors after the SEC had already required those projections removed from one fund's official offering circular in 2018. The case was revived on appeal, certified as a class action in March 2026, with trial set for March 2027 — unresolved as of now.

Net worth claimsHis publicly stated net worth ($1.3–2.6B) appears to conflate money he manages for investors (~$4B in assets under management) with his own personal wealth; independent estimates put his actual net worth closer to $600–750M. He does not appear on Forbes' or Bloomberg's billionaire indexes.

10X as rhetoric, not researchNo study establishes that a literal 10x multiplier outperforms other multipliers — it's a branding device layered on top of the real, well-supported idea that specific, ambitious goals beat vague ones.

More detail (money rules, closes, business model)
  • 40/40/20 rule — 40% of gross income to taxes, 40% to savings/investment, live on the remaining 20%, far more aggressive than a standard 50/30/20 budget.
  • Good debt vs. bad debt — debt used to buy cash-flowing assets (like investment real estate, targeted around 65% loan-to-value) is "good"; consumer debt is "bad." He explicitly rejects Dave Ramsey's zero-debt philosophy as, in his words, "middle-class thinking."
  • Never buy a primary home, never use a 401(k) — both genuinely contrarian, debated positions, not mainstream financial consensus; he has also promoted rolling 401(k) funds into his own real-estate deals, which independent analysts have called self-serving since it forfeits any employer match.
  • Sales closes — the alternative-of-choice close (never a yes/no question, always a choice between two "yes" outcomes), the minor-point close (build a chain of small agreements), and the take-away close (signal the deal might not still be available).
  • Scientology — Cardone is a longtime, publicly acknowledged member and donor (reportedly $18M+ over 20 years). Worth knowing as context for the absolutist, no-excuses tone of his content, separate from whether any individual piece of advice is sound.
