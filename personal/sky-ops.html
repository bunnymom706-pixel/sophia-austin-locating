    The menu

    This is a menu, not an assignment. Twenty-five habits is why you feel behind. Three is why you'll feel ahead.

    For the ADHD, specifically

    These aren't discipline fixes. They're workarounds for a brain that runs on interest and urgency instead of importance.

    • Build a launch pad by the door. One tray: keys, lockbox code card, tape measure, business cards, phone charger. You stop losing eleven minutes a day to searching, and you stop starting every showing already flustered.
    • Set a two-minute timer and give yourself permission to quit when it rings. Initiation is the wall, not effort. You will almost never quit at two minutes — but you have to genuinely be allowed to.
    • Put "leave the house" in your calendar 15 minutes before you actually need to leave. Time blindness isn't fixable by trying harder. It's fixable by lying to your calendar.
    • Body-double the boring stuff. FaceTime a friend on mute while you do follow-ups or expense receipts. The presence of another person is a stimulant your brain accepts.
    • Put your hardest money task inside your Adderall peak, not your admin. Prospecting calls and follow-ups go in the good hours. Laundry and email do not deserve your best neurochemistry.
    • Add novelty on purpose. New coffee shop, new playlist, new route to a showing. Boredom is a symptom for you, not a character flaw — treating it as a scheduling input works better than white-knuckling through it.
    • Keep one doom box. One bin for everything that has no home. Ten minutes on Sunday, no guilt. This alone ends most of the "my space is chaos" spiral.
    For the credit card debt

    Paying off a 26% card is a guaranteed 26% return. Nothing in your investment account will ever beat it.

    • Call each card and ask for a lower APR. Say: "I've been a customer since [year], I'm carrying a balance, and I'd like to request an APR reduction." Ten minutes, and roughly a third of the time it works.
    • Delete the card numbers from Apple Pay, Safari autofill, and every saved checkout. Not willpower — friction. You'll rediscover you didn't want most of it.
    • Keep only a $1,000 buffer, then send everything else at the debt. Saving at 4% while borrowing at 26% is paying for the privilege of feeling safe.
    • Check whether you qualify for a 0% balance transfer card — and if you do, put the payoff deadline in your calendar the same hour you open it. That card is a countdown, not a break.
    • Draw the payoff, don't just track it. Same 100-square grid, but each square is $100 of debt erased. Coloring in destruction feels better than coloring in accumulation.
    For running 1099 + a new W-2 at the same time

    This combination is where 22-year-olds get destroyed in April. It's also where you have an unfair advantage most locators don't know about.

    • Open a second checking account today and route every commission into it. Business in, business out. This single move is the difference between an hour of tax prep and a lost weekend.
    • Use your new job's W-4 to cover your 1099 taxes. Line 4(c) lets you request extra withholding from each paycheck. Set it high enough to cover the locating income and you may never write a quarterly estimated check at all. Ask your new employer's HR to help you set it — it takes one form.
    • If you'd rather pay quarterly: Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15. Four calendar events, set to repeat, right now.
    • Turn on automatic mileage tracking. You drive Austin all day. At roughly 70¢ a mile, a normal locating week is a meaningful deduction you are currently throwing away.
    • Snap every receipt into one photo album called "Taxes." No sorting, no naming. Sorting is a January problem; capturing is a today problem.
    • End each workday with a five-minute pipeline touch. Three follow-ups, one line each in your CRM. Commissions come almost entirely from the second and third contact, and that is exactly where an ADHD brain drops the thread.
    For living with him now

    Money fights are almost never about money. They're about being surprised.

    • Put a 20-minute money date on the calendar, same time weekly. Bills, what's coming, what's tight. Scheduling it means it never has to happen mid-argument at 11pm.
    • Decide the split once, in writing. Straight 50/50, or proportional to income. Either is fine. Undefined is what kills people. Yours is variable commission income — proportional usually protects you in slow months.
    • Keep money that neither of you has to explain. A set amount each, no justification required. This one thing prevents most cohabiting-couple resentment.
    • Tell him the debt number out loud. Not for permission. Because carrying it silently in a shared apartment is heavier than carrying it alone.
    Read this on the bad days

    When it feels pointless

    The problem isn't that you're not hitting goals. It's that you set them 365 days away and then asked your brain to care today.

    You're 22, holding down a new job and a commission business, in a new apartment, with debt you're actively facing instead of ignoring. That is not someone who's failing. That's someone with no feedback loop — you're doing the work and getting no signal back, and a brain that runs on dopamine reads "no signal" as "pointless."

    So shrink the horizon until the signal is visible. Thirteen weeks. Eight checkboxes. One square. That's the entire strategy — everything above is just plumbing for it.

    One honest note: if the flat, nothing-matters feeling shows up even on days you do hit your three, that's worth mentioning to whoever prescribes your Adderall. Stimulants can flatten mood, and "life feels boring and pointless" is real clinical information, not a personality trait you're stuck with. Say it in those words at your next appointment.
