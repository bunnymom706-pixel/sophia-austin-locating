# The Science

Short version of why each piece of the system exists. Real studies only. If a piece has
weak evidence, it says so.

## Morning sunlight, no sunglasses
- **Wright et al., 2013, Current Biology.** A week of camping with only natural light shifted
  people's internal clocks about two hours earlier and lined them up with sunrise. Outdoor light
  is the strongest signal your body clock gets.
- **Burns et al., 2021, Journal of Affective Disorders.** UK Biobank, about 400,000 people. Each
  extra hour of daytime outdoor light was linked to lower odds of depression, better mood, easier
  waking, and less tiredness.
- Why no sunglasses: the clock-setting cells in the retina need the light to reach them.
  Windows and sunglasses cut most of it.

## Focus reps
Each step maps to a study.

- **Squats first.** Chang et al., 2012, Brain Research, meta-analysis of 79 studies. A short
  bout of exercise gives a small but real boost to thinking and self-control right after.
  Pontifex et al., 2013, Journal of Pediatrics: 20 minutes of exercise improved attention and
  task performance in kids with ADHD.
- **Say "starting" and "done."** Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006, Advances in Experimental Social
  Psychology, meta-analysis of 94 studies. Naming the exact moment you start ("when X, I do Y")
  roughly doubles follow-through compared to just intending to.
- **Phone on Do Not Disturb.** Stothart, Mitchum, and Yehnert, 2015, JEP: Human Perception and
  Performance. Just hearing a notification, without checking it, hurt task performance as much
  as actually using the phone. Ward et al., 2017, Journal of the Association for Consumer
  Research: the phone sitting on the desk, face down and silent, still lowered working memory.
  Out of sight is measurably better.
- **30 seconds fixed gaze.** Weakest evidence in the set. The link between holding the eyes on
  one point and mental focus comes from attention research showing where the eyes rest and
  where attention goes are wired together. There is no clean trial of "stare 30 seconds, then
  work." Treat it as a cheap ritual that marks the start, not a proven lever.
- **Stop on the timer.** Ariga and Lleras, 2011, Cognition. Attention on one task drops over
  time. Short, planned breaks reset it. Ending on the clock, not on feeling, keeps the rep
  the same size every time, which is what makes it repeatable.
- **Eyes closed 1 to 2 minutes after.** Dewar et al., 2012, Psychological Science. Ten minutes
  of quiet wakeful rest after learning improved memory a week later versus doing another task.
  Wamsley, 2019, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, reviews the same effect: rest right after work
  helps the brain file what it just did.

## Streaks, missed days, and "done beats good"
- **Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology.** 96 people tracked a new daily
  habit. Median time to automatic was 66 days, range 18 to 254. Missing one day did not
  measurably hurt habit formation. That is the whole basis for "missed day, run today's reps,
  no catch-up."
- **Amabile and Kramer, 2011, Harvard Business Review, "The Power of Small Wins."** From
  12,000 daily diary entries: the single strongest driver of motivation on a given day was
  making progress on meaningful work, even tiny progress. Logging one line a day is that.

## Speed to lead
- **Oldroyd, McElheran, and Elkington, 2011, Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of
  Online Sales Leads."** 1.25 million leads across 2,241 companies. Firms that reached a lead
  within an hour were about 7 times more likely to qualify it than firms that waited even one
  more hour, and over 60 times more likely than firms that waited a day. Your "under 5 minutes"
  rule is the fast end of that curve.

## Money system
- **Thaler, 1999, Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, "Mental Accounting Matters."** People
  treat money differently based on the bucket it sits in. Separate tax, buffer, and salary
  accounts use that instead of fighting it.
- **Thaler and Benartzi, 2004, Journal of Political Economy, "Save More Tomorrow."** Automatic
  deductions set up in advance beat willpower every month. Savings rates roughly tripled.
  That is why the 25 percent and 10 percent come off every deposit automatically.

## Weight plan
- **Morton et al., 2018, British Journal of Sports Medicine.** Meta-analysis of 49 trials.
  Protein intake helps keep and build muscle up to about 1.6 g per kg of body weight. For you
  that is roughly 120 to 130 g a day, which is where the 130 g target comes from.
- **Steinberg et al., 2015, Obesity, "Weighing every day matters."** People who weighed daily
  lost more and kept it off better than those who weighed less often. The 7-day average is
  used because day-to-day water swings of 2 to 4 pounds are normal and mean nothing.
- **Nedeltcheva et al., 2010, Annals of Internal Medicine.** Same calorie deficit, two sleep
  conditions. With 5.5 hours of sleep, 55 percent less of the weight lost was fat and more was
  muscle, compared to 8.5 hours. Sleep is part of the diet.
- **Raynor and Epstein, 2001, Psychological Bulletin.** More food variety at a meal means more
  eaten. Same breakfast and same lunch every day removes both the decision and the overeating.

## Lights Out routine
- **Phillips et al., 2017, Scientific Reports.** Irregular sleep timing, even with the same
  total hours, was linked to a later body clock and worse performance. A fixed routine at the
  same time nightly is the fix. The tiles are there so the routine runs without deciding.
